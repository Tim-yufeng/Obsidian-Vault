
Now I will explain the business model of Liberty Media, Formula 1.

For Liberty Media, Formula 1 is not just a racing championship. It is more like a sports intellectual property business. Its core asset is the Formula 1 World Championship, including commercial rights, media rights, and global brand value.

Formula 1 makes money from several channels. First, host cities and race promoters pay fees to hold a Grand Prix. Second, TV networks and streaming platforms pay for media rights. Third, global brands pay for sponsorship and advertising. Formula 1 is attractive to brands because it is linked with speed, technology, luxury, and international attention.

It also earns money from premium events, such as Paddock Club and VIP experiences. Finally, it licenses its brand for games, apparel, collectibles, and other products.

The important point is that these revenue streams support each other.

When Formula 1 has a larger audience, its media rights become more valuable, because broadcasters are willing to pay more for live sports content. A larger audience also attracts more sponsors, because brands want global exposure.

At the same time, greater media exposure makes hosting a race more attractive for cities and promoters. A Grand Prix can bring tourism, media attention, and city branding. Also, when the Formula 1 brand becomes stronger, fans are more likely to buy merchandise or pay for premium experiences.

So the business model creates a positive cycle: audience growth increases commercial value, and this value can be reinvested into better events, better content, and further global growth.
